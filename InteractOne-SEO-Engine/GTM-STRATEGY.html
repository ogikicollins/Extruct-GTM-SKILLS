01

The situation

InteractOne is a 27-year, roughly 18-person, 100 percent onshore Cincinnati eCommerce agency founded and run by Brian Dwyer. It has quietly served 250-plus brands with an average client tenure above five years.

It has just repositioned from a generalist Magento and Shopify shop to "B2B commerce built to perform" for manufacturers and distributors with complex catalogs. The positioning is right. The demand generation to match it does not exist yet.

The SEO service is genuinely differentiated on a four-way intersection few competitors hold: developers and SEOs under one roof, programmatic SEO for large catalogs, B2B and industrial catalog fluency, and senior-only onshore delivery.

It is also under-marketed and capacity-constrained. Today the entire outbound function is Amanda, sending emails between account-management work. There is no SDR team, and no one owns pipeline as a job.

GTM sellability score — 74 / 100

Two constraints shape every decision below. The delivery team is small, so the engine is throttled to 3 to 5 qualified opportunities per month, not 50. And the engine is built inside your Salesforce and dotdigital from day one, so nothing has to be migrated back to you later. You own it because it never leaves.
02

What we actually sell

Do not sell "SEO." The word is a commodity and the category is crowded with bigger names. Sell a specific, urgent outcome to a specific buyer, entered through a specific first step.

Sub-offer A · Recovery

"Your replatform cost you 20 to 40 percent of organic traffic and nobody owns the fix." Proof: Aladdin Temp-Rite recovered 25 percent organic growth and 66 percent total traffic after a Magento to Adobe Commerce move.

Sub-offer B · Scale

"You have 8,000 product and category pages and 400 of them rank." Programmatic SEO is the capability content agencies cannot cheaply copy, because fixing it is a development job.

Positioning line: "The SEO team that reads your Magento logs, not just your blog."

Three gaps to close before launch

  • Productize the entry. Free Programmatic SEO Teardown, then a fixed-fee Technical & Migration Audit at $3,500 to $7,500, then the retainer.
  • Add a light AEO line. Schema, entity optimization, answer-formatted content for AI search at plus $900 to $2,500 per month. It is a 2026 buyer question and a competitor wedge.
  • Refresh the proof. Only three quantified SEO case studies exist. Pull fresh 90-day numbers from three long-tenured clients and instrument every new engagement for a results story.

Pain hypotheses

Six testable angles. Each is a detectable signal plus a list query plus an email angle plus the proof to cite. Layer 6 tracks them by audit-close rate, graduates winners above 4 percent reply, and retires anything below 2 percent over 50 sends.

Belief
Companies that replatformed in the last 6 to 12 months lost 20 to 40 percent of organic traffic to migration errors nobody owns.
Signal
BuiltWith shows a platform switch in the last year and Ahrefs organic traffic is down 15 percent-plus since that date.
Angle
"The drop is almost always a handful of technical misses in the migration, not a content problem."
Proof
Aladdin Temp-Rite, plus 25 percent organic after Magento to Adobe Commerce.

Belief

The engine

A six-layer machine that runs the same at 3 AM as 3 PM. Signal-triggered, not schedule-triggered. It lives inside your Salesforce and dotdigital: every account, sequence, call log, task and reply is a native record your team can already see. Two dedicated SDRs run the daily activity. Nothing is drafted or sent without a person.

1

Intelligence

ICP, three personas, six hypotheses, competitor displacement map, do-not-contact list. The frozen reference for every downstream layer.

2

Activation

List queries per hypothesis, enrichment columns, a dollar-value traffic-gap figure on every account, a 100-point tiering rubric, contact discovery and verification. Loaded into Salesforce as campaign members.

3

Outreach

SDR-led multichannel: cold calls first, email through dotdigital, LinkedIn touches, and the teardown hook. Three sequences by persona, call scripts and objection handling, a pre-send review gate. Brian Dwyer co-signs the top accounts.

4

Pipeline

Reply and call-outcome routing, a seven-input lead-scoring model in Salesforce, a two-hour speed-to-lead SLA, an auto-generated pre-call dossier before every meeting.

5

Close

The audit-first close, seven deal stages, post-call nurture, a three-tier retainer proposal, a seven-objection bank, Day 0 to 90 onboarding, five expansion paths, a referral engine.

6

Optimize

Weekly report from Salesforce, eight feedback loops, hypothesis graduate-or-retire rules, a weekly signal monitor that feeds new accounts back into Layer 2.

The teardown is the hook the SDRs lead to

Cold calls and multichannel touches all point at one low-friction offer: a free 3 to 5 minute Loom walking through three real issues on the prospect's live site with a rough dollar figure for the traffic gap, and the line "yours to share internally" so it gets forwarded to the economic buyer. Booking a call is step two, never the opening ask.

90-day roadmap

We build and staff the engine; your team gives access, approves the ICP, and joins the closing calls. Because the build is inside Salesforce and dotdigital, there is no external tooling to warm up. The critical-path item is Salesforce and dotdigital access in week one.

Weeks 1–2

Foundation

  • Salesforce + dotdigital access, objects and fields mapped
  • Sign off ICP, personas, positioning, do-not-contact list
  • Build the target universe and six segments
  • Enrich, tier, verify contacts, load as campaign members
  • Two SDRs onboarded to the account and the offer
  • Call scripts, sequences, teardown workflow built in-platform
Weeks 3–4

Launch

  • Record teardown Looms for the first 20 accounts
  • Review-gate pass, InteractOne approves go
  • Launch H1 only: SDR calls + dotdigital email + LinkedIn
  • SDRs on the 2-hour speed-to-lead SLA
  • Dwyer starts one LinkedIn teardown post a week
Weeks 5–8

Scale + operate

  • Add H4, H2, H6 to the rotation
  • Multi-thread the top accounts
  • First weekly reports and hypothesis verdicts
  • First paid audits sold and delivered
  • Capture before-state on any signed retainer
Weeks 9–13

Optimize + expand

  • Retire the weakest hypothesis, reallocate SDR time
  • Add H3 and H5
  • First retainers converting from audits
  • Referral asks on Day-60 green-health clients
  • Decide whether to add a third SDR
