# Mock Campaign Prep Framework — Stage 3 of Their Process
Built 2026-08-07. Their pipeline: Loom -> interview 1 -> short mock campaign -> interview 2 -> offer.
This is a framework to run when the mock campaign brief actually arrives — not a campaign built blind
today, since you don't have their brief yet. Save this file and re-open it when the assignment lands.

The JD is explicit about what they're screening against: "not someone who parrots buzzwords," "finds
the angles and lead sources others miss," "reasons through a go-to-market." A generic, template-shaped
mock campaign fails this even if every section is technically present. Judge your own output against
that bar before you submit it.

---

## What almost certainly gets evaluated

Given the JD's own language, expect the mock campaign to be scored on:
1. Whether the targeting logic has a specific, defensible reason — not "companies with 50-200
   employees in SaaS," but a reason tied to buying behavior or a trigger event.
2. Whether the lead sourcing goes beyond an Apollo export — this is the "no database has these leads"
   test. Even in a short mock, show at least one non-obvious source or signal.
3. Whether the copy reads human. No em-dash-heavy AI cadence, no "I hope this finds you well," no
   value-prop-then-CTA template shape. Read every line out loud before submitting.
4. Whether you show your reasoning on deliverability (domain warmup, sending volume ramp, list
   hygiene) even in a small mock — not just "then we send emails."
5. Whether you can articulate what you'd measure and what you'd change based on early results —
   this mirrors exactly what you did on the real Structural diagnostic report.

## Step-by-step method to run when the brief arrives

**1. Re-read the brief twice before opening any tool.** Identify the actual buying trigger the client
sells against, not just their category. (Structural example: not "customer support software" — "teams
whose support volume is repetitive enough that a deterministic bot beats a human, but bespoke enough
that no off-the-shelf bot already owns it.")

**2. Find one non-obvious signal or source, even for a small mock.** Options depending on the brief:
job postings (hiring signal), recent funding/leadership change, a public complaint pattern (review
sites, Reddit, G2), a regulatory trigger, a competitor's customer visibly unhappy in public. This is
the single highest-leverage move in the whole exercise — it's the exact thing they said they're
looking for ("the more non-obvious, the better").

**3. Write copy against that signal, not against the persona.** Every line should read like it
couldn't have been sent to anyone else on the list. Use the Structural rewrite as the template for
the *method*, not the words: kill generic rhetorical-question opens, anchor on something real and
sourced, make the CTA binary and low-effort for the reader.

**4. State the deliverability plan explicitly, even briefly.** Domain/mailbox setup, warmup timeline,
sending volume ramp, verification step before send. A one-paragraph version is fine for a short mock —
omitting it entirely reads as inexperience given who's evaluating.

**5. Close with a falsifiable metric and a stated "what I'd change" — before you have real results.**
State what number would tell you the campaign is working, what number would tell you it's a targeting
problem vs. a messaging problem (the exact distinction the Structural diagnostic made), and what
you'd test next. This shows the diagnostic instinct, not just the build instinct.

**6. Cut anything that sounds like marketing copy about yourself.** No "leveraging AI-powered
workflows to drive pipeline." Describe what you built in plain, specific sentences.

## Before submitting

- Read it once as if you were the founder who's been doing this solo — would this actually save you
  time, or is it a longer version of what you'd have written yourself?
- Cut length. A tight, sharp mock beats a comprehensive one — this is a small assignment, not the
  final deliverable.
